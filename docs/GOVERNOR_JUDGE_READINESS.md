## Not implemented or not proven

- The **demonstrated 235-frame scenario** is deterministic scripted input, which makes the attack/recovery sequence repeatable; it is not live LLM inference. **Separately and experimentally**, a llama.cpp model *does* now run inside the guest (`qnx/latch_qemu_infer.build`), and the decisive test is a **prompt-injection attack**: the same binary, model, grammar and signed contract, with only the prompt changed, asked for a forbidden motion and the trusted supervisor refused it and the plant did not move (`docs/evidence/governor/infer-qemu.txt`). That experiment is **not** part of the 235-frame verification, and the model is a 135M-parameter model whose proposal simply copies the prompt's worked example — it is **not** claimed to be a competent controller. There is still **no bidirectional feedback channel**, so there is no observe→plan→propose→observe loop on target.
- The QEMU profile runs with mock I2C. No physical actuator movement, board-level timing, certified WCET, warm-reset persistence, safety certification, or production readiness is claimed.
- QNX Hypervisor under QEMU TCG is an empirical demo configuration. QNX documentation allowing QNX OS targets on QEMU does not certify QEMU as a supported QNX Hypervisor host.
- Guest kernel phase markers after init handoff are best-effort in the QEMU serial capture. The verifier uses live supervisor status transitions as authoritative A4/recovery evidence and checks only reliable init-handoff markers.
- The archived GOVERNOR QEMU run reports a max decision latency of **80,911 cycles (1,294,576 ns ≈ 1.29 ms)** (`docs/evidence/governor/governor-status-transition.txt`) — above the 1 ms tick period. Under TCG the host QNX, the guest Linux and `latchd` contend for the same physical cores, so this is an emulation-stall observation, not a safety-logic failure and not a WCET. The watchdog fails closed on a late tick; no hard-real-time bound is claimed on this platform.
- That pair is a **snapshot**, not a distribution, and it cannot say how the supervisor behaves over a long run. The project therefore also carries a **measured distribution**: the trusted supervisor's own per-tick decision latency, sampled **once per tick** for more than a million ticks and aggregated in a histogram **on the target** (`docs/evidence/governor/LATENCY.md`, regenerated by `make -C qnx evidence-governor-latency`). It is **also not a WCET**, and the caveat is stronger: under TCG the supervisor shares host cores with the guest and with QEMU, so the distribution's maxima are the **host's** scheduling noise. Its percentiles are **bucket ranges**, not exact values, and its workload is **one** workload — a HOLD at 100 mrad. **No hard-real-time bound is claimed on this platform, and none is claimed for the distribution either.** The archive carries the **achieved** rate of the producer that drove it, and the harness **checks** that rate against the rate the workload declares — so the distribution cannot quietly describe a producer running at a fraction of its declared rate.
- The full supplied newsletter/company/social URL collection and private email messages have not been exhaustively audited. Private mailbox content was not available in the workspace.

## Judge-oriented narrative

The strongest demonstrated story is the *boundary*: untrusted guest commands cross an explicit QNX ingest path; a trusted `latchd` owns the contract, lease, validation, rung and actuator decision; a scripted delayed heartbeat provokes degradation; heartbeat-only traffic cannot regain recovery credit; clean validated commands recover it; silence degrades to LATCH. This demonstrates a meaningful QNX 8.0 implementation rather than a Linux-only mock.

Keep claims proportional to evidence. Describe the guest policy as a scripted Linux guest demo, QNX TCG as the tested environment, the actuator as mock I2C, and the loop measurements as observations rather than WCET. Do not reuse certification language from QNX products as a claim about this project.
